# Micronation: design plan (not built yet)

Status: stage 1 built (2026-10-07), stages 2 to 5 open. Design agreed, agreed in discussion on 2026-10-07. Build in stages, simulate and commit each one.

## Unlock
Net worth of about $500M or more, and you own land (estate, private island, sea platform, remote territory or an artificial island).

## Core loop
Each year the ruler makes 1 to 3 decisions. Decisions shift five national meters (0 to 100): Prosperity, Order, Freedom, Legitimacy, Reputation. Citizens, investors and foreign governments care about different meters, so there is no single best strategy. Law changes cost money and take time to settle.

## 1. Founding
Sites: estate within a country, private island, sea platform, remote territory, artificial island. Each differs in cost, credibility and risk.
Identity presets: libertarian haven, tech and crypto utopia, eco commune, luxury tax haven, cultural republic, or custom. Each sets starting laws, the type of citizen attracted and a signature event line.
Symbols: name, flag, motto, anthem, national day. They feed legitimacy and tourism.

## 2. Government
Ruler style: king or queen, president, council, direct vote. Elections are an optional law (accept a loss, rig it at scandal risk, or abolish them at a legitimacy cost).
Laws (sliders, with cost and delay): income, corporate and wealth tax, tariffs; immigration; speech and press; privacy and surveillance; regulation (business, finance, AI, drugs, gambling, pharma); welfare, healthcare, education; currency (own coin, dollar, basket, crypto); citizenship rules.
Institutions with a quality level and running cost: courts, police, border service, central bank, tax office, university, hospital, port. Weak institutions cause fraud, corruption and slow growth.

## 3. Citizens
Groups: entrepreneurs, remote workers, investors, retirees, scientists, artists, activists, workers, opportunists, criminals. Each has wants, wealth and loyalty, and moves in or out as laws change.
Named citizens (6 to 10 at a time) with a role, loyalty, secret and story: engineer, journalist, rebel, spy, investor, a friend from the player's life.
Cabinet: ministers for finance, security, foreign affairs, health and culture. Good ministers add yearly bonuses, bad or disloyal ones cause scandals. Friends from the friends system can ask for posts.

## 4. Economy
Revenue: citizen and corporate taxes, visas and citizenship sales, tourism, port and customs, licensing, national companies, sovereign investments.
Costs: infrastructure, security, utilities, healthcare, education, debt service.
National treasury is separate from the player's money, with its own balance sheet, debt and credit rating (AAA to default).
Projects (multi-year): airport, deep-water port, power plant, data centre, free trade zone, hospital, university, resort strip, financial centre, launch pad, desalination.
Economic zone focus (finance, tech, tourism, manufacturing, research, trade): growth bonus plus a matching vulnerability.

## 5. Treasury: deposits and withdrawals
Deposit:
- Donation: raises Legitimacy and Reputation slightly, no repayment.
- Loan to the nation: the player sets the rate, the treasury repays from revenue, the player loses it on default.
- Project investment: a share of that project's profit.
Withdraw:
- Salary: a fixed yearly amount set by law. A very high salary lowers Legitimacy.
- Dividends: only from national companies the player owns a share of.
- Loan repayment.
- Direct grab: possible, but it is corruption. It adds to a hidden corruption score, leading to leaks, investigations, coup attempts or outside prosecution, and hits Legitimacy, credit rating and Reputation.
Guardrails: a minimum reserve of about 3 months of costs must stay in the treasury. Auditors, a free press and strong courts make grabs riskier. Personal and national finances are shown side by side.

## 6. Outside world
Recognition ladder: ignored, tolerated, trade partner, diplomatic contacts, recognised by 1 country, recognised by several, full state with a seat in a global body. Each rung needs reputation, institutions and a favour or trade deal.
Diplomacy: treaties, trade deals, embassies, lobbying, sports and culture diplomacy, hosting a summit, foreign campaign donations (risky).
Threats: host-country pressure, sanctions, blockades, tax-haven blacklisting, hostile media, currency attacks, cyber attacks, espionage.
Rival micronations and larger neighbours as named characters.

## 7. Events (multi-step chains)
Coup attempt, spy scandal, referendum, refugee wave, natural disaster, banking panic, whistleblower leak, famous visitor or summit, Olympic bid, world-first experiment (UBI, 4-day week, AI judge, car ban), discovery (oil, minerals, wreck), revolution when Legitimacy and Freedom are both low.

## 8. Endings and score
Thriving state (passes to heir with a legacy bonus), peaceful handover, decline and merger, collapse, takeover by a foreign power.
Nation score: population, prosperity, happiness, recognition and years survived. It adds to the life legacy.

## 9. Links to other systems
Heirs inherit the nation or a government seat. Political experience helps in real-country races. Companies can set up in the free zone for tax savings, with scandal risk. Friends become ministers or critics. A rival billionaire may attack the nation. Owned media lifts or sinks Reputation.

## 10. Military (later stage, after the core is stable)
Optional and switchable: a "peaceful mode" toggle keeps the nation diplomacy only.
Levels of defence: none, border service, defence force, private military contractors, mercenaries, or a protection pact with a larger power (paid yearly, costs independence).
Army stats: size, equipment, training, morale, readiness, and a Deterrence meter that lowers the chance of being targeted.
Costs and laws: yearly budget, conscription or volunteer force, arms purchases (embargo risk), veterans' benefits.
Risks: a strong army can stage a coup if Legitimacy is low or the commander is disloyal. Keep generals loyal with pay, honours and oversight.
Conflicts: border incidents, blockades, proxy skirmishes with rival micronations, invasion by a neighbour. Outcomes come from relative strength, terrain, alliances and leadership, with diplomatic off-ramps at each step.
Aggression: starting a war costs Reputation and Legitimacy, and may trigger sanctions. War economy raises costs and casualties, which hit Order and Legitimacy.
Not planned: nuclear weapons.

## UI plan
One Nation screen. Top: five meters, population, treasury. Tabs: Overview, Government, Citizens, Economy, Projects, Diplomacy, Events log (and Defence in the military stage). A yearly State of the Nation card summarises changes.

## Build stages
1. Founding, laws, meters, citizens, treasury (including deposits and withdrawals).
2. Economy, projects, credit rating, zones.
3. Outside world: recognition, diplomacy, threats, rivals.
4. Events, cabinet, named citizens, endings, dynasty links.
5. Military and conflict (optional mode).
